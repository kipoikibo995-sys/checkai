How ChatGPT writing gives itself away

Large language models such as ChatGPT write by picking likely next words. That leaves patterns a person rarely produces all at once:

  • Unusually predictable wording. Each word is close to what a language model would expect next. This is the strongest signal, and the one our AI model measures.
  • Buzzwords and stock phrases such as "delve into", "a testament to", "plays a crucial role", "seamless" or "in today's fast-paced world".
  • Stock transitions at the start of sentences: "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally", "In conclusion".
  • Even sentence lengths. People mix short and long sentences; unedited AI text is often strikingly regular.
  • Little personal voice. Few contractions, opinions, specific memories or first-person details.

None of these alone proves anything. Together, they make a text read as machine-made, and that is what the checker scores.

How the check works

The score combines two parts. The larger part (70%) comes from two small language models from the SmolLM2 family that run in your browser. They use the Binoculars method: they measure how predictable each word is, then compare that with how predictable a model would expect the text to be. Dividing the two keeps naturally predictable topics, such as technical or formal writing, from being flagged just for being plain. The other 30% comes from the six writing-style signals listed above.

Because everything runs on your device, the first time you turn on the AI model your browser downloads it once (about 730 MB for the Accurate model, or 275 MB for the Lite model on phones). After that it loads from your browser in a few seconds.

Two honest caveats. HC3 comes from the 2022 version of ChatGPT; newer models write less predictably, and on a small set of 11 texts mostly from 2026 models the Accurate model caught only 6. And text that was edited, paraphrased or run through a "humanizer" is much harder for any detector to catch.

Who uses a ChatGPT detector, and for what

  • Teachers use it as a first filter on essays, then talk with the student or look at drafts before drawing any conclusion.
  • Editors and content managers check freelance articles and spot paragraphs that read as raw AI output, so they can ask for a rewrite with real examples and sources.
  • Hiring teams look at cover letters and writing samples, where generic AI phrasing hides what the candidate actually thinks.
  • Writers check their own AI-assisted drafts to find the passages that still sound generic and rewrite them in their own voice.

In every case the useful output is the same: the highlighted phrases and the plain-language reasons show where a text reads as machine-made, which is far more actionable than a single percentage.

Tips for a reliable result

  • Check at least 150 words. Results under 120 words are unreliable.
  • Check English text. German works only as an experimental option; other languages are not supported.
  • Look at the highlighted phrases and sentences, not only the percentage.
  • Never treat one score as proof. Ask for drafts or version history if it matters how a text was written.

Frequently asked questions

Is this ChatGPT checker free?

Yes. It is free, needs no account and has no daily limit. The AI model downloads once into your browser, and after that every check runs on your own device.

Can it detect GPT-4, GPT-5 and newer ChatGPT models?

It looks for patterns common to large language models, so it can flag text from newer ChatGPT versions too. Newer models write less predictably, though, so expect it to miss more of their text than of older ChatGPT output.

Does it also detect Claude or Gemini?

Yes, in the same way: it does not look for one product's fingerprint, but for the predictability and style that most AI models share.

Can students or writers be wrongly flagged?

Yes, occasionally. In our tests the Accurate model flagged 2 of 57 human answers. Formal, plain writing and writing by non-native speakers can look more predictable. Never use a detector score as the only evidence.

Is the text I paste stored?

No. The check runs inside your browser and nothing is uploaded. Only a short list of your recent checks is kept in your own browser, and you can clear it.
