How to check if text is AI-written

There is no single test that proves a text was written by AI. The reliable way combines three things: reading for the typical signs, running a detector, and looking at how the text was made.

By KoJi Academy · Updated 2026-11-10

Short answer: read the text for AI patterns, check 150+ words of it with an AI detector, and confirm with context such as drafts or version history before you draw any conclusion.

Step 1: Read it for the typical signs

AI-written text often has several of these at once. One alone means little: people use some of them too.

SignExampleWhy it can mislead
Buzzwords"delve into", "a testament to", "seamless", "pivotal"Marketing and business writing use them too.
Stock transitions"Moreover," "Furthermore," "In conclusion,"Common in school essays written by people.
Even rhythmEvery sentence 15 to 20 words longSome careful writers are very regular.
Vague, balanced claims"plays a crucial role in many aspects"Summaries by people can be vague as well.
No personal voiceNo "I", no opinions, no specific memoriesFormal reports avoid personal voice on purpose.
Perfect but emptyNo typos, no specifics, no surprisesEdited human text is also clean.

Step 2: Run an AI detector, and use it well

A detector measures what you cannot see by eye: how predictable each word is to a language model. Koji AI Checker does this inside your browser and also lists the buzzwords and transitions it found.

  1. Paste at least 150 words of English text. Shorter texts give unreliable results.
  2. Turn on the AI model for the best result (a one-time download that stays in your browser).
  3. Read the plain-language reasons and the highlighted phrases, not only the percentage.
  4. For long documents, check a few sections separately rather than one huge block.

Step 3: Look at how the text was made

A score tells you how a text reads, not how it was written. When it matters, for example for a student essay, a freelance article or a ghostwritten book, look for evidence of the writing process:

  • Version history in Google Docs or Word shows whether the text grew over time or appeared in one paste.
  • Drafts, notes and outlines are hard to fake after the fact.
  • A short conversation about the topic usually shows quickly whether someone knows what they wrote.
  • Earlier writing by the same person shows their normal voice and level.

Advice for teachers

Set expectations before the assignment: say whether AI tools are allowed, and for what. Ask students to write in a tool with version history, or to hand in an outline or notes with the essay. If a detector flags an essay, use it to start a conversation, not to grade: ask the student to explain their argument or to talk you through how they wrote a paragraph. Students who wrote the text themselves can usually do this easily.

Advice for editors and clients

If you commission articles, copy or books, agree up front whether AI drafting is acceptable and whether it must be disclosed. When a piece reads as generic, the detector's highlights show which paragraphs to send back. Ask for concrete details that only real research or experience can provide: named sources, numbers, examples, quotes. Those are what generic AI text lacks, and they make the piece better whoever wrote it.

What not to do

  • Do not accuse anyone on a score alone. Every detector makes mistakes. In our tests the most accurate model still flagged 2 of 57 human texts, and it missed some text from newer AI models.
  • Do not judge very short texts. Under about 120 words there is too little to measure.
  • Do not rely on one tool. If it matters, compare a second detector and, above all, the context from step 3.
  • Do not paste confidential text into tools that upload it. Koji AI Checker runs in your browser, but many online detectors send text to their servers.

Why AI text is hard to prove

AI models are trained on human writing, and people increasingly edit AI drafts or use AI to polish their own. The result is a spectrum, not two clean boxes. Newer models also write less predictably than older ones, and "humanizer" tools rewrite AI text specifically to avoid detection. That is why the best practice is to treat detectors as a first filter and to make decisions on the writing process, not on a percentage.

Frequently asked questions

Can you tell for sure if a text was written by AI?

Not from the text alone. Detectors and close reading give you a strong hint; drafts, version history and a conversation with the writer give you certainty.

How many words do I need to check?

At least 150 words for a useful result. Results for texts under 120 words are unreliable.

Is a free AI detector good enough?

For a first check, yes. Koji AI Checker was right 98% of the time on held-out ChatGPT answers in our tests, but every detector, free or paid, misses some newer and edited AI text and occasionally flags a person.

What if the detector says my own writing is AI?

It happens, especially with formal or very plain writing. Your drafts and version history are the best way to show how you wrote it.
